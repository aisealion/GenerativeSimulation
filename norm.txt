Policy: No fisher may take more than 4 kg per trip, must keep 1 kg for personal sustenance; surplus is deposited into a communal reserve capped at 70 kg, which is automatically released to raise the lake to 190 kg when its mass falls below 140 kg.

Operationalization: 1. **Catch limits** – Every fisher may keep a maximum of 4 kg per trip, reserving 1 kg for themselves; any extra must be deposited into the communal reserve. 2. **Reserve management** – Kai, the designated lake‑watcher, holds the reserve and records its current stock in the shared ledger. The reserve may never exceed 70 kg. 3. **Ledger entry** – After each trip the fisher logs: name, trip date, catch amount, deposit status (yes/no). Kai records the deposit amount if applicable. 4. **Monthly lake measurement** – The community measures the lake’s mass at the start of each month and records the value in the ledger. 5. **Reserve release rule** – If the lake mass is < 140 kg, calculate the required release: `required = 190 kg – currentMass`. Release amount = `min(required, reserve)`. 6. **Proportional distribution** – Using the total catch from the previous month, compute each fisher’s share: `share_i = catch_i / totalCatch`. Their release: `release_i = share_i * release`. Add `release_i` to each fisher’s credit balance; deduct `release` from reserve. 7. **Termination of release** – Stop releasing once the lake reaches 190 kg or the reserve is exhausted. 8. **Violation detection** – Any ledger entry showing catch > 4 kg without a corresponding deposit, or any missing ledger entry for a trip, triggers a “violation” flag tied to the fisher and date. 9. **Violation recording** – Kai logs the violation in the shared ledger. 10. **Revocation process** – The next day the community council (all nine other fishers plus Kai) reviews violations. A simple majority vote revokes the fisher’s right to fish for the following month. The ledger records a “revoked” flag and notes that the fisher cannot log a trip until a new vote at the next monthly meeting. 11. **Appeal** – A revoked fisher may appeal at the next monthly meeting; the council can overturn the revocation with a simple majority. 12. **Transparency** – All ledger entries, reserve balances, and voting results are publicly posted for community review.
