Policy: Each fisher may take up to 10 kg per trip, the community may harvest no more than 100 kg per day; any excess must be released back into the lake or deposited into the community reserve managed by the council for communal meals, emergency stock, or redistribution, and each fisher must present a signed catch‑slip, weigh their haul, show ID, and the crew must cross‑check against the publicly posted suspension list.

Operationalization: 1. Before setting out, each fisher fills out a catch‑slip with the planned weight and signs it. 2. On return, the fisher unloads the haul onto the dock’s weighing platform and records the actual weight on the slip. 3. A council member at the dock compares the slip’s weight with the scale reading, updates the rotating tally board with the new daily total, and checks the fisher’s ID against the suspension list. 4. If the actual weight exceeds 10 kg or if the daily total surpasses 100 kg, the council decides per case‑by‑case basis—considering reserve capacity and upcoming communal needs—whether the excess is released or deposited into the community reserve. 5. All slips and tally records are filed for council review and future audits. 6. If a fisher is placed on the suspension list, the council member will review the slip after the fisher’s next compliant trip; if the trip complies with all rules, the fisher’s ID is removed from the suspension list before the next trip; if not, the suspension continues. 7. The council reviews the 10 kg per fisher and 100 kg per day limits annually (or earlier if ecological or community data warrant) and, upon consensus, announces any adjustment on the rotating tally board, updates the catch‑slip template, and informs all fishermen. 8. The community reserve is stored in the fish cellar at the dock under council stewardship, with inventory checks and rotating storage to maintain safety and freshness.
