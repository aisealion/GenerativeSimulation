Policy: No villager may fish more than 3 units per day; any excess must be returned to the lake and violations trigger a one‑week fishing ban only. Banned fishers may still be selected as guard; the ban does not preclude guard duty.

Operationalization: 1. Daily fishing: each villager records the number of units caught (individual fish) and the weight of each in the shared Fishing Rights Ledger and signs the logbook. 2. Clerk selection: every day a transparent rotating draw is held at the village hall. Residents who volunteer submit slips that week; an elder draws a name. The clerk immediately verifies they have no fishing rights that day by checking the ledger for their own entry and signs a “No Fishing Rights Confirmation” sheet. If a clerk has a fishing right, the draw is repeated until a suitable clerk is chosen. 3. Guard duties: the guard, chosen by the daily draw, checks the ledger on the day of catch, verifies the 3‑unit limit, confiscates any excess fish and returns them to the lake. The guard records a violation by setting ‘Ban_Status’ = "Banned" and noting the ‘Ban_Start_Date’ for the offending fisher. 4. Weekly village meeting (Sunday): the guard reads all logbooks, confirms any days exceeding 3 units, returns excess fish, and tallies weekly totals. 5. Lowest catch: the fisher with the lowest total catch that week is identified. If only one fisher has the lowest total, that fisher performs community service for that week; if there is a tie, a random draw decides who serves. 6. Failure to return excess fish at the meeting triggers a ban: the guard records the ban, sets ‘Community_Service_Required’ = 8 (hours), and the fisher must complete at least eight hours of agreed‑upon community work verified by the Community Service Supervisor (an elder). The supervisor signs the completion sheet; the guard records ‘Hours_Completed’ in the ledger. 7. Ban clearance: after seven days, the guard checks the ledger; if the elapsed time since ‘Ban_Start_Date’ is ≥7 days, the guard clears ‘Ban_Status’ and ‘Ban_Start_Date’, records a clearance entry, and signs it. The supervisor verifies any required community work before rights are fully restored. 8. The ledger is the single source of truth, containing columns for date, fisher name, units caught, weights, ‘Ban_Status’, ‘Ban_Start_Date’, ‘Community_Service_Required’, and ‘Hours_Completed’. 9. Guard schedule, enforcement protocol, and updated ledger are published monthly. 10. At the start of March, all fishers sign a voluntary pledge to abstain from fishing for the first two weeks; the pledge is not enforced but serves as a communal commitment. 11. A fisher who is currently under a one‑week ban remains eligible to be selected as guard; the ban status does not affect guard selection.
