Policy: Each fisher may keep up to 12 kg per trip, with at least 1 kg reserved for personal sustenance; excess over 12 kg is deposited into the community pot and recorded. Weekly council meetings allocate the pot proportionally to shortfalls below 12 kg, with penalties for non‑compliance and a graduated fishing ban for repeated violations.

Operationalization: 1. Each trip: fisherman records total catch. If catch ≤12 kg, keep all for personal use (minus 1 kg for sustenance). If catch >12 kg, keep 12 kg (1 kg sustenance + 11 kg personal), deposit excess (catch‑12 kg) into pot, produce a deposit slip. 2. Deposit slip is handed to the council member at the end of the day; the council confirms the amount. If slip missing or amount short, council deducts 1 kg from the fisher’s personal catch as penalty and records the shortfall. 3. The communal ledger (shared ledger visible to all) records: day, fisher name, total catch, deposit amount, penalty, net personal catch, and current pot balance. 4. At Sunday evening council meeting, the council: a) totals each fisher’s net personal catch for the week; b) identifies fishers with net catch <12 kg and computes each shortfall (12 kg – net catch); c) sums all shortfalls to get total shortfall; d) obtains current pot balance P; e) if P ≤ total shortfall, distributes P proportionally to shortfalls (A = (shortfall ÷ total shortfall) × P) and sets pot balance to 0; f) if P > total shortfall, distributes total shortfall proportionally as above, leaves surplus pot balance in ledger for future use. 5. Penalties: for each violation of the 12 kg limit (catch exceeding 12 kg), the fisher is barred one day; second violation bars two days; third three days; up to five days. The council records the bar days and prevents the fisher from entering the lake during that period. 6. If a fisher repeatedly fails to deposit excess or submit slip, the council imposes the 1 kg penalty and may increase the bar days accordingly. 7. All actions are logged immediately in the communal ledger; the ledger is reviewed by council each meeting and made available to every fisher. 8. The council itself is formed by one elected representative from each household, elected by simple majority vote annually; representatives meet Sunday evenings, maintain the ledger, enforce deposits, penalties, and bar days, and decide on pot redistribution.
