Policy: Each fisher may take only one trip per day and may catch no more than two units of fish; excess fish must be returned to a communal reserve and repeated violations trigger bans as recorded in the community ledger.

Operationalization: 1. The eldest fisher, chosen monthly by the council of village chief, senior fisher, market stall owner, and head of fishing families’ association, acts as the volunteer reviewer.\n2. Every fisher signs in at departure and out at return, recording time, trip count, and catch count using a net or basket with a built‑in counter or numbered slots.\n3. The volunteer reviews the ledger daily at 6 pm, verifies each fisher’s reported catch against the physical counter, and ensures no fisher has logged more than one departure per day or more than two units.\n4. If a mismatch or over‑catch is found, the fisher must immediately correct the ledger; a second discrepancy results in a violation.\n5. Violations increment a fisher’s Violations counter in the ledger: 1st = 1‑week ban, 2nd = 2‑week ban, 3rd+ = 4‑week ban, capped at 4 weeks.\n6. The volunteer enforces the ban by withholding boat keys, locking the boat, and noting the ban on the ledger. Keys are handed from the village steward to the reviewer when a ban is imposed, and returned to the steward when the ban ends.\n7. After the ban period ends, the volunteer resets the fisher’s Violations counter to 0.\n8. All ledger entries, violations, and bans are displayed on the communal board for transparency.\n9. Fiscers may appeal a ban by attending a brief community meeting, but must first demonstrate correct recording procedures.\n10. The volunteer reviewer also maintains the communal reserve: each returned surplus is logged in the ledger under a Return column, and the reserve basket is counted and recorded daily at 6 pm to keep the inventory accurate.\n10.2 The volunteer records the ban start date in the ledger; at 6 pm daily the volunteer checks whether the ban period has elapsed (1 week for 1st violation, 2 weeks for 2nd, 4 weeks for 3rd+). Once elapsed, the volunteer lifts the ban, returns keys to the steward, resets the fisher’s Violations counter to 0, and updates the ledger and communal board to reflect the change.
