Policy: Each fisher may take no more than three units per trip and must leave at least one unit in the lake; any excess is returned immediately, with a pre‑trip check that prohibits fishing if the lake holds fewer than one unit.

Operationalization: After each trip, the fisher records the number of fish caught (each fish counts as one unit) in a personal ledger and reports the figure to the observer before sunset. The observer updates the communal ledger with the fisher’s total, marks the reserved unit(s) deposited into the lake keeper’s reserve, and ensures the lake keeper records the lake’s remaining stock. Before a fisher departs, the observer confirms with the lake keeper that the lake’s remaining stock is ≥1 unit; if not, the trip is denied. If a fisher exceeds the 3‑unit limit or the lake drops below one unit during a trip, the excess fish is returned to the lake and the fisher is flagged. Violations are counted by the observer: two consecutive violations or three separate violations within a 30‑day month trigger a skip‑day, which is imposed by a majority vote of the five elders (at least three votes). Repeated violations incur an annual fine set by the Community Council, deducted from the fisher’s next communal contribution (one unit per day). All records are kept in the communal ledger and reviewed nightly by the lake keeper and council. The observer is the village watchman, chosen by majority vote of the elders each season; the lake keeper is appointed annually by the council based on experience and integrity. The month for tallying violations is the 30‑day cycle of the communal lunar calendar.
