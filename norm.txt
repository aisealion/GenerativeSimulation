Policy: Each fisher may take a maximum of 0.75 units per trip; surplus fish returned are redistributed to those below the 1‑unit daily consumption need, with any remaining shortfall met by the communal reserve or collective sharing; violations are penalized by the community elder after a consensus vote.

Operationalization: 1. Every fisher carries a calibrated net‑scale that shows the catch weight immediately after setting the net. 2. If the scale shows more than 0.75 units, the fisher must return the excess to the lake before finishing the trip; all returned fish are counted as surplus. 3. After each fishing round, the designated elder collects the returned surplus, weighs it on communal scales, and records the total in the communal ledger. 4. The scribe, elected by consensus, maintains the ledger in the village meeting house and posts a copy on the wall board; during the daily meeting the ledger is read aloud and any villager may inspect it. 5. The elder calculates each fisher’s deficit relative to the 1‑unit survival need and distributes the surplus proportionally to those deficits, recording the allocation on the ledger. 6. If the surplus is insufficient to cover all deficits, the elder draws from the Reserve Balance (tracked as a line item in the ledger); if the reserve is insufficient, the elder orders surplus catchers to contribute part of their next day’s catch or provide an extra labor shift, recording the adjustment on the ledger. 7. When a violation is discovered, the elder convenes a quick meeting of all active fishers, discusses the excess amount, and the community votes on an appropriate penalty (monetary contribution or extra fishing shift). 8. The elder records the penalty in the ledger and collects any monetary contribution into the communal pot; if the penalty is an extra shift, the elder assigns the fisher to the next scheduled trip and notes this on the ledger. 9. The elder ensures no fisher may leave the next trip until the penalty is recorded, maintaining transparency and social enforcement.
