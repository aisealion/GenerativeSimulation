Policy: Each fisher may take up to 12 kg per trip and must set aside 1 kg to a community pot; if lake stock falls below 100 kg the catch limit is reduced to 8 kg. The pot‑keeper is elected monthly by secret ballot, records contributions, and allocates 60 % of the pot to maintenance and 40 % to emergency aid, with a 20 % reallocation allowed in a serious emergency after Maintenance‑Aid Committee approval.

Operationalization: 1. **Catch limits** – Every fisher may haul a maximum of 12 kg per trip (8 kg if lake stock <100 kg) and must hand over 1 kg of that haul to the community pot. 2. **Pot‑keeper election and term** – The pot‑keeper is chosen each month by secret ballot among all active fishers; the position may not be held for more than one month. 3. **Ledger and reporting** – The pot‑keeper collects the daily 1 kg, records each fisher’s contribution in a communal ledger posted on the notice board daily, and prepares a monthly usage report. 4. **Allocation rules** – 60 % of the pot funds maintenance (nets, boats, docks), 40 % funds emergency aid. 5. **Serious emergency reallocation** – Defined as any event threatening life, health or immediate livelihood within 48 hours. The pot‑keeper may shift up to 20 % from maintenance to aid only after the Maintenance‑Aid Committee (3 volunteers elected monthly) votes in favour. The reallocation is recorded, posted on the board, and all fishers receive a written bulletin. 6. **Maintenance‑Aid Committee** – Elected monthly by secret ballot, reviews written requests with receipts, votes by majority on each request, and approves spending. 7. **Audit pair** – Two fishers elected monthly by secret ballot meet weekly to reconcile the pot‑keeper’s ledger against each fisher’s private log. 8. **Discrepancy handling** – If a discrepancy exceeds 5 % of total pot contribution or cannot be explained with receipts within three business days, the audit pair declares it irreconcilable, reports the issue to the community, and initiates removal procedures. 9. **Removal of pot‑keeper** – Upon an irreconcilable discrepancy, the community convenes a general meeting. A simple majority of all active fishers (≥50 %) constitutes quorum. Any fisher can trigger the meeting by posting a written notice on the board three days prior, or the pot‑keeper may trigger it if an audit finds an irreconcilable discrepancy. A simple majority vote of those present decides removal; the pot‑keeper must resign within one month, and a new pot‑keeper is elected by secret ballot. 10. **Daily compliance** – Each fisher keeps a private log of catch and 1 kg contribution; the pot‑keeper posts the communal ledger daily for transparency. 11. **Community oversight** – All fishers may audit the ledger, review the monthly report, and call special votes if needed. This framework balances individual fishing rights with communal stewardship and ensures transparent, accountable use of lake resources.
