Policy: Each fisher may take no more than 10% of the lake’s current stock per trip, capped at 10 kg, and the community’s total weekly catch may not exceed 20% of the lake stock at the week’s start; excess must be donated to the communal store and accompanied by 2 hours of community service, and if the lake stock falls below 150 kg, all fishing stops for four weeks until the stock recovers to at least 150 kg, after which the council authorizes a resume.

Operationalization: Fishing proceeds as follows: 1) Before each trip, the fisher consults the most recent weekly lake stock estimate in the communal ledger; the per-trip limit is 10 % of that stock, capped at 10 kg. 2) After each trip, the fisher records the actual catch weight in the bound communal ledger (and the digital backup) and signs it; the ledger keeper verifies the entry. 3) The council, composed of rotating active fishers, the village elder, and a neutral observer, reviews the ledger every Monday. 4) If a fisher exceeds the per‑trip limit, the excess weight is donated to the communal store and the fisher must perform 2 hours of community service. 5) The council sums all weekly catches and compares the total to 20 % of the lake stock at the week’s start; if the total exceeds that limit, the excess is donated to the communal store, all contributing fishers perform 2 hours of community service, and fishing is suspended for one week. 6) Lake stock is estimated weekly by the fishery officer using a 50‑meter seine net in a predetermined area; the officer records total weight and area sampled, divides to estimate stock, and logs the figure in the ledger. 7) If the estimated stock falls below 150 kg, the council orders a four‑week fishing stop; after four weeks the council re‑measures the stock, and if it is at least 150 kg, they announce fishing may resume via the ledger and radio. 8) Roles such as fishery officer and ledger keeper are elected by rotating nomination and show‑of‑hands each month; the fishery officer performs the weekly survey, and the ledger keeper verifies entries. 9) All fishers may view the ledger; only the keeper may modify or approve entries. 10) The council ensures compliance by checking ledger entries and issuing penalties as described.
