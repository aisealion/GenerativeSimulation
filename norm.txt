Policy: Each fisher may take a maximum of 4 kg per trip, and the community may not remove more than 25 % of the lake’s current biomass in any rolling 30‑day period. Excess fish over either limit are forfeited to a communal pool managed by the council and redistributed later for communal benefit.

Operationalization: Daily logs: each fisher records catch weight at landing; the council updates a rolling 30‑day ledger to compute each fisher’s total and the community’s total removals. 4 kg per‑trip limit: any weight above 4 kg is immediately confiscated and transferred to the communal pool. 25 % of lake biomass limit: if the community’s cumulative removal over any rolling 30‑day window exceeds 25 % of the current lake biomass, the excess is forfeited to the pool. Storage: forfeited fish are sealed in clean barrels at the communal facility, protected from spoilage. Council: composed of the most experienced fishers plus a few village elders, elected monthly by majority vote for a three‑month term; they oversee logs, enforce limits, and manage the pool. Redistribution: at the first Saturday after each 30‑day logging period, the council meets to review the pool ledger. Using a need index (1 point per child, 1.5 per adult in need, 1 per elder), they calculate each eligible member’s share; the pool is divided proportionally and distributed in the communal kitchen for full visibility. Contingency: if the pool cannot satisfy total indexed need, the council may request voluntary donations from fishers who had surplus forfeited fish, ensuring equitable support. Record keeping: all logs, forfeitures, and redistributions are recorded with date, weight, and recipients for transparency.
