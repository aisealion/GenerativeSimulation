Policy: No fisher may take more than 5 kg per trip and the community may not remove more than 25 % of the lake’s current biomass in any calendar month; excess catch is returned immediately and added to a 10 % communal reserve.

Operationalization: At the start of each month, the rotating ledger keeper conducts a calibrated gill‑net survey (or uses the previous month’s measurement if not possible) to record the lake’s baseline biomass in the ledger. Each fisher signs a log for every trip, noting weight, date, time, and next scheduled trip date on a shared calendar. The ledger keeper verifies the 5 kg per‑trip limit with a witness; if a fisher exceeds it, the excess is returned, logged as “returned to lake” and credited to the communal reserve. At the end of the month the keeper sums the signed logs chronologically; if the cumulative removal first exceeds 25 % of the baseline, the fisher whose trip caused the exceedance receives a warning recorded in the ledger and a skip marker added to their next scheduled trip. The skip marker is enforced by the ledger keeper acting as watch‑man: they withhold gear at the start of the marked trip and issue a public reminder. Failure to respect the skip leads to a second offense vote at the monthly meeting; the sanctioned fisher must skip the next scheduled trip and perform 3 hours of lake‑cleanup work, recorded as a penalty in the ledger and added to the communal reserve. The monthly meeting also reviews the 10 % communal reserve calculation (10 % of the total monthly removal after all excesses have been returned) and updates the community on the current reserve balance. The community votes each month to confirm or adjust the 25 % cap and any changes to sanctions, and the approved rule is recorded in the minutes and the ledger.
