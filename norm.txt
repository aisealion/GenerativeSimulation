Policy: Each fisher may keep up to 2 units of fish per day; any surplus is deposited into the communal reserve in the iron barrel (or a secondary container if the barrel is full), with all deposits and withdrawals signed by the cook and sealed by the steward.

Operationalization: 1. Roles: The cook and steward are elected by a simple majority of fishers at the start of each fishing cycle and may be re‑appointed or rotated by community vote.
2. Fishing trip: The fisher logs the total catch on the shared ledger.
3. Retention and deposit: The fisher keeps up to 2 units for personal use. If the iron barrel has space, the surplus is deposited into the barrel; if the barrel is at capacity, the surplus is placed in a secondary communal container on the lakebank, logged on the ledger, and signed by the steward.
4. Sealing: The steward seals the iron barrel (and the secondary container) until the end of the day.
5. Consumption: After eating, the fisher records his daily consumption on the ledger; the cook signs to certify the amount.
6. Short‑fall: If a fisher’s consumption falls below 1 unit, he submits a short‑fall request on the ledger. The steward compares it with the signed consumption, authorises a withdrawal from the reserve up to the shortfall, debits the reserve balance, and logs the transaction.
7. Non‑fisher withdrawals: Non‑fisher villagers may submit withdrawal requests in the same manner; each may withdraw up to 1 unit per day if reserve funds are available.
8. Violations: If a fisher fails to deposit surplus fish, the steward confiscates the missing portion, deposits it into the reserve, imposes a penalty of 0.2 units per un‑deposited unit, logs this penalty as a separate ledger entry, and increments the fisher’s violation counter.
9. Penalties: The penalty amount is added to the communal reserve and recorded on the ledger.
10. Suspension: After three recorded violations, the council of fishers votes—requiring a simple majority of members present—to suspend that fisher’s rights for two days or until all penalties are paid and compliance is demonstrated.
11. Restoration: After suspension, the fisher must submit proof of penalty payment and a corrected deposit log. The steward audits the ledger entries, the cook verifies signatures, and the council votes to restore rights.
12. Transparency: All deposits, withdrawals, consumptions, violations, penalties, suspensions, and restoration decisions are logged on the shared ledger for all villagers to view.
13. Physical reserve: The reserve is stored in a sturdy iron barrel on the lakebank, sealed when not in use; the steward opens it only for authorized deposits or withdrawals and updates the ledger immediately.
14. Secondary container: When the iron barrel reaches capacity, surplus fish are stored in a secondary communal container, sealed by the steward, and each overflow entry is logged on the ledger with the fisher’s name, amount, and steward’s signature.
