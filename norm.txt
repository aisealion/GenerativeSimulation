Policy: Each fisher may keep up to 2 units of fish per day; any surplus must be deposited into the communal iron barrel (120 units), then the secondary container (60 units), and finally a temporary bin if both are full, with a penalty of 0.2 units per un‑deposited fish.

Operationalization: 1. Roles: The cook and steward are elected by a simple majority of fishers at the start of each fishing cycle (one day). They may be re‑appointed or rotated by community vote.\n2. Daily Cycle: One whole day of fishing, from dawn to night when the cook signs the final ledger entry.\n3. Fishing Trip: The fisher logs the total catch on the shared ledger with timestamp.\n4. Retention and Deposit: The fisher keeps up to 2 units for personal use. If the iron barrel has space, the surplus is deposited there; if the barrel is full, surplus is deposited into the secondary communal container on the lakebank; if both are full, the fisher hands the surplus to the steward, who places it in a temporary communal bin on the lakebank.\n5. Overflow Handling: The steward logs each transfer and imposes a penalty of 0.2 units per unit of fish not deposited into the barrel or secondary container.\n6. Sealing: At the end of the day the steward seals the iron barrel and the secondary container (and the temporary bin if used).\n7. Consumption: After eating, the fisher records daily consumption on the ledger; the cook signs to certify the amount.\n8. Short‑fall: If consumption falls below 1 unit, the fisher submits a short‑fall request on the ledger. The steward authorises a withdrawal from the communal reserve up to the shortfall, debits the reserve balance, and logs the transaction.\n9. Non‑fisher withdrawals: Non‑fisher villagers may submit withdrawal requests in the same manner; each may withdraw up to 1 unit per day if reserve funds are available.\n10. Violations: If a fisher fails to deposit surplus fish, the steward confiscates the missing portion, deposits it into the reserve, records a penalty entry, and increments the fisher’s violation counter on the ledger.\n11. Penalties: The penalty amount is added to the communal reserve and recorded on the ledger.\n12. Suspension: After three recorded violations, the council of fishers votes—requiring a simple majority of members present—to suspend that fisher’s rights for two days or until all penalties are paid and compliance is demonstrated.\n13. Restoration: After suspension, the fisher must submit proof of penalty payment and a corrected deposit log. The steward audits the ledger entries, the cook verifies signatures, and the council votes to restore rights.\n14. Transparency: All deposits, withdrawals, consumptions, violations, penalties, suspensions, and restoration decisions are logged on the shared ledger for all villagers to view.\n15. Physical Reserve: The reserve is stored in a sturdy iron barrel on the lakebank, sealed when not in use; the steward opens it only for authorized deposits or withdrawals and updates the ledger immediately.\n16. Secondary Container: When the iron barrel reaches capacity, surplus fish are stored in a secondary communal container, sealed by the steward, and each overflow entry is logged on the ledger with the fisher’s name, amount, and steward’s signature.\n17. Temporary Bin: When both barrel and secondary container are full, surplus fish are placed in a temporary communal bin on the lakebank, logged on the ledger, and will be moved to the reserve as soon as space frees.
