Policy: Each villager may keep up to 10 units of fish per day, with 10 % of every catch deposited into a communal reserve; excess beyond 10 units is returned to the lake; a rotating council of five elected villagers verifies the ledger, redistributes surplus to bring short‑fallers to a 1‑unit threshold, and authorizes reserve use only in declared emergencies or by majority vote.

Operationalization: 1. After fishing, the fisher weighs the total haul. 10 % of the weight is poured into the communal reserve bucket located at the dock, overseen by the dockkeeper, and recorded on the dock ledger. 2. The remaining 90 % is weighed; if it exceeds 10 units, the excess is dropped back into the lake via a return chute. 3. The dockkeeper immediately records a ‘Returned’ entry in the ledger specifying the exact weight returned that day. 4. The fisher records total catch, reserve contribution, and kept amount on the dock ledger and signs it. 5. A council member, rotating daily according to the pre‑determined list of the five elected villagers, verifies the weight against the ledger entry and signs it. 6. At day’s end, the council tallies: (a) villagers with <1 unit; (b) surplus from over‑10 catches; (c) all voluntary donations. 7. The council votes by simple majority on how to redistribute the surplus pool: the surplus is divided proportionally to each short‑falling villager’s deficit until every shortfalling villager reaches 1 unit. 8. Any remaining fish in the pool is added to the communal reserve. 9. If the reserve is insufficient to cover any remaining shortfall, the council may temporarily allow affected villagers to keep more than 10 units—just enough to meet the deficit—while ensuring the lake stock stays above the minimum safe threshold of 200 kg. 10. Excess fish beyond the 1‑unit threshold after the temporary increase is returned to the lake at the next day’s ledger. 11. The dockkeeper updates the lake stock after all returns and records the new stock on the ledger. 12. The council may audit the dockkeeper’s stock record for consistency. 13. Emergency declaration criteria: lake stock <200 kg, >20 % of villagers short‑falling, or no net catch for three consecutive days. A simple majority vote by the council triggers reserve release in emergencies. 14. Reserve use: the council releases from the reserve exactly the amount needed to bring every short‑falling villager to 1 unit; any remaining reserve stays in the pot for future use or emergency aid. 15. All decisions, ledger entries, and reserve allocations are recorded in the dock ledger for transparency.
