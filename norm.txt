Policy: Each fisher may take up to 0.75 units per trip, with a maximum of two trips per day; any excess is returned as surplus, redistributed to cover deficits up to 1 unit per fisher, with remaining shortfall met by the Reserve Balance, and when the Reserve is insufficient, surplus catchers must donate part of their next day's catch or provide extra labor; monetary penalties are collected into a communal pot, whose disbursement is decided by consensus of the council (all active fishers present, the elder, and the scribe), and all actions are recorded in the communal ledger.

Operationalization: 1. Each fisher carries a calibrated net‑scale that shows the catch weight immediately after setting the net.
2. If the scale shows more than 0.75 units, the fisher must return the excess to the lake before finishing the trip; all returned fish become "Surplus Returned".
3. After each fishing round, the elder collects the Surplus Returned, weighs it on communal scales, and records the total in the communal ledger under a line item "Surplus Returned".
4. The elder computes the safety threshold by multiplying the number of active fishers by 0.75 and compares it to the remaining lake stock.
5. If the remaining stock exceeds the threshold, the elder authorises second trips for that day:
   5a. The elder convenes a quick meeting of all active fishers present, invites volunteers for a second trip, and if volunteers are fewer than permits, allocates remaining permits based on deficit priority.
   5b. The elder records the names granted permission in the ledger under "Second Trip Permits".
6. The scribe maintains the ledger, posts a copy on the wall board, and during the daily meeting reads the ledger aloud; any villager may inspect it.
7. The elder calculates each fisher’s deficit relative to the 1‑unit survival need and distributes the surplus proportionally to those deficits; this allocation is recorded in the ledger.
8. If the surplus is insufficient to cover all deficits, the elder draws from the Reserve Balance; the draw is recorded in the ledger.
9. If the Reserve Balance is also insufficient, the elder orders surplus catchers to donate part of their next day’s catch (0.25 units per 0.25 unit deficit) or provide an extra one‑hour labor shift; the adjustment is recorded in the ledger.
10. Monetary penalties for violations are collected into a communal pot; the pot balance is recorded in the ledger under "Pot Balance".
11. When pot usage is required, the elder calls a council meeting (all active fishers present, the elder, and the scribe); the council votes; the decision is recorded in the ledger under "Council Minutes" (date, attendees, proposal, vote, allocation).
12. Pot disbursements are recorded under "Pot Allocation – Subsidy" or "Pot Allocation – Equipment".
13. Any violation discovered triggers the elder convening a quick meeting, discussing the excess, and the community voting on a penalty (monetary or extra shift); the elder records the penalty in the ledger, adds monetary contributions to the pot, or assigns the fisher to the next trip if the penalty is an extra shift.
14. All ledger entries are posted daily on the wall board for transparency.
