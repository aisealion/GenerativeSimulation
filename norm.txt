Policy: Each fisher may take up to 0.75 kg per trip, a maximum of two trips per day; excess is returned to the lake, deficits are met by redistributed surplus, then by the Reserve Balance, and if still unmet by donation or extra labor; monetary penalties are collected into a communal pot and disbursed by council consensus; all actions are recorded in the communal ledger.

Operationalization: 1. Each fisher carries a calibrated net‑scale that displays catch weight immediately after setting the net. 2. If the scale shows more than 0.75 kg, the fisher must return the excess to the lake before finishing the trip; all returned fish become "Surplus Returned". 3. After each fishing round, the elder collects the Surplus Returned, weighs it on communal scales, and records the total in the ledger under "Surplus Returned". 4. The elder calculates the safety threshold by multiplying the number of active fishers by 0.75 and compares it to the remaining lake stock (measured by the elder using total catch weight and a standard volume‑area calculation; the result is recorded as "Remaining Lake Stock"). 5. If the remaining stock exceeds the threshold, the elder authorises second trips: 5a. The elder convenes a quick meeting of all active fishers present, invites volunteers for a second trip, and if volunteers are fewer than permits, allocates remaining permits based on deficit priority; 5b. The elder records the names granted permission in the ledger under "Second Trip Permits". 6. The scribe maintains the ledger, posts a copy on the wall board, and during the daily meeting reads the ledger aloud; any villager may inspect it. 7. The elder calculates each fisher’s deficit relative to the 1‑unit (1 kg) survival need and distributes the surplus proportionally to those deficits; this allocation is recorded in the ledger. 8. If the surplus is insufficient to cover all deficits, the elder draws from the Reserve Balance; the draw is recorded as "Reserve Draw". 9. If the Reserve Balance is also insufficient, the elder orders surplus catchers to donate part of their next day’s catch (0.25 kg per 0.25 kg deficit) or provide an extra one‑hour labor shift; the adjustment is recorded as "Donated Contribution" or "Extra Labor". 10. Monetary penalties for violations are collected into the communal pot; the pot balance is recorded as "Pot Balance". 11. When pot usage is required, the elder calls a council meeting (all active fishers present, the elder, and the scribe); the council votes; the decision is recorded as "Council Minutes" (date, attendees, proposal, vote, allocation). 12. Pot disbursements are recorded under "Pot Allocation – Subsidy" or "Pot Allocation – Equipment". 13. Any violation discovered triggers the elder convening a quick meeting, discussing the excess, and the community voting on a penalty (monetary or extra shift); the elder records the penalty in the ledger, adds monetary contributions to the pot, or assigns the fisher to the next trip if the penalty is an extra shift. 14. All ledger entries are posted daily on the wall board for transparency. 15. The Elder is the most senior fisher elected by a unanimous vote of all active fishers at the start of each day; the role may rotate annually. 16. The Scribe is chosen by a simple majority vote of all active fishers each season; the scribe serves until the next election or until a vacancy arises. 17. The Reserve Balance is a communal pool of fish kept in trust by the elder; it is replenished by a portion of Surplus Returned when lake stock is comfortably above threshold, and depleted only after deficits are met by surplus redistribution. 18. The quorum for council decisions is the presence of all active fishers, the elder, and the scribe; absence of any active fisher prevents the council from convening.
