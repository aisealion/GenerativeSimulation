Policy: No villager may fish more than 2 units per day, must abstain during the first two weeks of March, and any excess must be returned to the lake; violations trigger a one‑week fishing ban and mandatory community service.

Operationalization: 1. Each fishing day, every villager records the units caught in the shared Fishing Rights Ledger (paper or digital) and signs the logbook.
2. The guard is chosen by a transparent rotating draw held each day at the village hall: all residents who sign up as a volunteer that week submit a slip; the clerk—an elder with no fishing rights that day—draws one name. Anyone who has just served as guard is excluded for the next 7 days, ensuring rotation.
3. The guard checks each day’s logbook entries on the day of catch, verifies the 2‑unit limit, and if excess is reported, confiscates the extra fish and returns them to the lake. The guard records the violation in the ledger by setting ‘Ban_Status’ = "Banned" and noting the ‘Ban_Start_Date’.
4. At the weekly village meeting, the guard reads all logbooks, confirms any reported day exceeding 2 units, and the excess fish is returned at that meeting. The guard tallies the weekly totals.
5. The fisher with the lowest total catch that week is identified. If only one fisher has the lowest total, that fisher performs community service during the week; if there is a tie, the tied fishers undergo a random draw (coin flip or die roll) to determine who serves.
6. A fisher who fails to return excess fish at the meeting is subject to the same ban: the guard records the ban in the ledger, and the fisher must complete at least eight hours of agreed‑upon community work (e.g., net repair, dock cleaning, communal meals) verified by the Community Service Supervisor (an elder). The guard signs off the completion sheet, and after the one‑week ban expires, the fisher’s rights are restored.
7. The March abstention rule is enforced by the guard’s daily ledger check; any catch recorded in the first two weeks of March is flagged as a violation, triggering the same one‑week ban and community service.
8. The guard’s updates to ‘Ban_Status’ and ‘Ban_Start_Date’ become part of the shared state in the ledger. After seven days have elapsed, the guard clears ‘Ban_Status’ and restores fishing rights.
9. The community publishes the guard schedule, the enforcement protocol, and the updated ledger each month. All fishers sign a pledge at the start of March to abstain from fishing for the first two weeks.
10. The ledger, guard schedule, ban status, and community service records are the single source of truth for all enforcement actions, ensuring transparency and fairness.
