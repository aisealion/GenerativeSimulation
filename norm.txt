Policy: Each fisher may keep up to 1.2 units of fish per day; any excess must be deposited in the communal reserve. Consumption, deposits, withdrawals, and violations are recorded on a shared ledger signed by the cook, and the steward enforces deposits, penalties, and suspension rules with a council vote requiring a simple majority.

Operationalization: 1. Each day a fisher records his catch on the ledger; he may retain up to 1.2 units and must deposit any surplus into the iron barrel reserve on the lakebank. 2. After eating, the fisher logs his daily consumption on the ledger; the village cook signs off to certify the amount. 3. If consumption is below 1 unit, the fisher writes a short‑fall request on the ledger; the steward compares it with the signed consumption and authorizes a withdrawal up to the shortfall, debiting the reserve balance. 4. Non‑fisher villagers may also write a withdrawal request, sign it with the cook, and the steward grants up to their calculated shortfall, subject to reserve availability. 5. All deposits, withdrawals, consumptions, and any violations are recorded on the shared ledger so every villager can see the reserve status. 6. If a fisher fails to deposit surplus fish, the steward confiscates the missing portion, deposits it into the reserve, and imposes a penalty of 0.2 units per unit of un‑deposited fish. 7. A violation is counted each time the steward records a missing deposit. After three violations, the council of fishers votes—requiring a simple majority of members present—to suspend that fisher’s rights for two days or until all penalties are paid and compliance demonstrated. 8. All violations, penalties, and suspensions are logged on the ledger for transparency. 9. The reserve is physically stored in a sturdy iron barrel on the lakebank, sealed when not in use; the steward opens it only for authorized deposits or withdrawals and updates the ledger immediately.
