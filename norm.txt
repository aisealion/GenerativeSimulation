Policy: Each villager may take at most one unit of fish per day; any excess must be released, and violations are fined one fish unit per excess unit and require community service.

Operationalization: 1. Daily log: Every fisher records on a paper sheet the date, net used, number of fish taken (must be ≤1 unit), the weight measured in the communal bin, and a statement that any excess fish were released. The fisher signs the sheet and a nearby community member (or spouse) signs as witness. 2. Communal bin: All caught fish are deposited into a communal bin that contains a calibrated scale. The bin is sealed for a 24‑hour period and weighed daily; the weight is recorded in the shared ledger. 3. Weekly council meeting (every Sunday at 7:00 pm in the village hall): Elder Tane collects any fines from fishers who exceeded the quota, reviews the daily logs and compares the logged weight with the bin’s recorded weight, and records each fine in the ledger. 4. Penalties: For each extra unit beyond the quota the fisher must return the excess fish, pay a fine of one fish unit per excess unit, and perform one day of community service. 5. Community service log: The fisher signs a community‑service log that lists the date, activity (e.g., dock repair, kitchen clean‑up, school aid), and hours spent. Elder Tane verifies the log at the meeting, asks a brief confirmation question, and records the service in the shared ledger. 6. Audits: Random audits are conducted by the council member or an appointed verifier. A fisher may be asked to demonstrate the release of excess fish, show the sealed bin, or provide a photo. If a fisher fails to provide a signed log, the bin weight exceeds the claimed 1‑unit, or an audit reveals non‑compliance, the fine is doubled and an extra day of community service is added. All audit findings are entered in the ledger for transparency.
