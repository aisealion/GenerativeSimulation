Policy: Each fisher may take up to 15 kg per trip; excess must be returned to the lake. A shared ledger records intended and actual catch, excess fish, and reserve allocation: 70 % for maintenance and 30 % for fishers below a 1 kg daily food reserve, split equally if multiple. The council audits monthly, enforces forfeiture, and maintains the reserve pit.

Operationalization: 1. Trip procedure: before departure record intended catch. After return, weigh actual catch. 2. If actual >15 kg, excess = actual‑15 kg; return excess immediately to the marked reserve pit and record in ledger. 3. Ledger entry: date, intended, actual, excess returned, net catch kept. Submit ledger entry within two hours. 4. Reserve pool: each month the community pool is divided 70 % for maintenance and 30 % for food reserve. 5. Maintenance: any expenditure on nets, boats, docks, cleaning is logged in the ledger under a "Maintenance" section with cost. Total monthly maintenance cost is subtracted from the 70 % pool. If insufficient, take remaining shortfall from the 30 % pool. 6. Food reserve: for each fisher, sum all net catches on a calendar day. If total <1 kg, the fisher is flagged. At month end, all flagged fishers receive an equal share of the remaining 30 % pool. If no one is flagged, the 30 % pool stays in reserve. 7. Council: composed of seven fisher‑representatives plus the fishery officer. Meet monthly to audit the ledger, verify compliance, and issue forfeiture notices. 8. Forfeiture enforcement: a fisher who fails to comply (catch >15 kg, not returning excess, or failing to record timely) loses fishing rights for the next trip; other fishers and the council chair deny access to boats or spots. 9. Pit maintenance: council assigns a keeper each month; fishers must keep the pit tidy. 10. Reporting: the council publishes a monthly summary to all fishers showing maintenance costs, reserve allocations, and any forfeitures.
