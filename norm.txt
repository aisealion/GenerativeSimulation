Policy: Each fisher may take at most 10 kg per trip; any excess over 10 kg is automatically deposited into a communal reserve, and 5 % of the total haul must be deposited into that reserve and recorded in the communal ledger. The Reserve Officer—chosen by community vote among fishers who have completed at least three successful trips—will oversee deposits, lock the bin, and enforce bans for non‑compliance.

Operationalization: 1. Before departing, the fisher prepares a ledger entry noting the total catch weight. 2. If the catch exceeds 10 kg, the fisher transfers the excess weight directly into the reserve bin. 3. The fisher then calculates 5 % of the total haul and deposits that amount into the reserve bin. 4. At the dock, the Reserve Officer weighs the bin, records the total deposited weight, and updates the communal ledger with the fisher’s name, haul weight, excess transferred, and 5 % deposit. 5. The bin is secured with a padlock held by the Reserve Officer; the officer signs the ledger entry and locks the bin. 6. The Reserve Officer is selected by a simple majority vote at the monthly community meeting from fishers who have logged at least three successful trips (meeting the criteria of catching ≥1 kg, staying ≤10 kg, depositing the 5 % share, and recording the haul). 7. The role rotates every 30 days; the outgoing officer conducts a final audit, hands over the bin, ledger, and notes, and the new officer assumes responsibility. 8. If a fisher fails to deposit the 5 % share or record the haul, the Reserve Officer reports the breach and the community imposes a temporary fishing ban until the obligation is fulfilled. 9. After each rotation cycle, the community reviews the ledger for discrepancies; any found must be corrected before the next trip. 10. The reserve fund is used solely for community emergencies or to support those who cannot fish, and its balance is reviewed quarterly by the community council.
