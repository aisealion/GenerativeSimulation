Policy: Each villager may catch no more than 1.5 kg of fish per day; any excess must be returned to the lake or deposited into the communal pool.

Operationalization: At the end of each fishing day the fisher weighs the total catch on the communal hand‑scale, records the weight next to their name on the shared ledger, and signs it with the appointed recorder. If the weight exceeds 1.5 kg the fisher must drop the surplus back into the lake at the agreed spot or hand it over to the fish keeper; the surplus is recorded in the common pool ledger and stored in the communal granary. The recorder and the fish keeper are appointed by the village council; the ledger is kept in the council office with a backup in the recorder’s hut, and both safeguard it. The recorder checks the hand‑scale weekly and audits the ledger. Any discrepancy triggers a review by the fish keeper and council, resulting in mandatory return of surplus, forfeiture of excess, a fine of one fishing right, or a temporary seven‑day ban if the fisher has committed three or more infractions within a rolling 30‑day period. Infractions are tracked in the fish keeper’s ledger, counting only those days where the 1.5 kg limit was violated or the recording procedure was not followed. An appeal may be filed by a fisher within three days of a fine or ban by submitting a written request to the fish keeper; the council will hold a hearing within a week, record it with the recorder, and may uphold or overturn the penalty.
