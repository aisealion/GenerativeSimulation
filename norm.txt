Policy: Each fisher may keep up to 15 kg per trip; any surplus above 15 kg must be shared equally among the community, and failure to share incurs a 2 kg forfeiture, a second 2 kg forfeiture, and a one‑week ban on repeated non‑compliance. The communal pot, built from forfeitures, is used or redistributed by council‑led vote when it reaches 10 kg.

Operationalization: After each trip, the fisher brings the net to the shore weigh station where the appointed ledger keeper records the total catch. If the weight exceeds 15 kg, the keeper subtracts the surplus from the fisher’s personal account, records the amount, and distributes the surplus equally among the other fishers, noting each share in the communal ledger. The ledger is verified by a rotating guard who checks the weight and confirms entries after every trip, with a full audit every third trip. If a fisher fails to share surplus, the ledger marks an omission; on the next trip the fisher must surrender 2 kg (or the entire catch if < 2 kg) to the communal pot, with the transfer recorded as a debit to the fisher and a credit to the pot. A second omission triggers a 4 kg forfeiture (or the entire catch if < 4 kg) and, if still unfulfilled, a one‑week fishing ban enforced by the community, preventing access to gear until the ban expires and community approval is obtained. The communal pot, accumulated from forfeitures, is held in locked storage; when it reaches 10 kg it is reviewed at the monthly meeting, where the council of long‑time fishers leads a vote to decide its use or redistribution. The outcome is recorded in the ledger and communicated to all fishers in writing.
