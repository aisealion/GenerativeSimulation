Policy: No fisher may harvest more than 10% of the lake’s current stock in a single trip, and the lake must retain at least 25% of its stock for natural regeneration; all catches are logged on the communal ledger, excess over 10% and a 10% catch‑tax fund the community reserve, and a temporary moratorium is imposed when stock falls below 25% and lifted only after two consecutive quarterly surveys confirm a stable or improving stock at or above 25%.

Operationalization: Operationally, the community monitors—fishermen elected each quarter by majority vote—conduct quarterly sonar surveys and compile catch data from all trips to estimate total stock and calculate each fisher’s 10% limit. Each trip’s catch is logged on the communal ledger; if a fisher exceeds the 10% limit, the excess and a 10% catch‑tax (each 1 kg of fish = 1 community credit) are transferred to a dedicated bank account that holds the community reserve fund. Fines of 20 community credits are levied by the monitors for any violation of the 10% limit. When the sonar surveys show the lake’s total stock has fallen below 25% of its estimated capacity, the monitors impose a temporary moratorium that remains in force until two consecutive quarterly surveys confirm that stock has risen to at least 25% and has not dipped below that threshold in either survey. The rotating committee of five members serves a one‑year term (renewable once), is elected by simple majority vote at the monthly meeting, and must hold a quorum of at least three members for any vote. The committee meets monthly to review the reserve balance, approve expenditures that must receive a simple majority vote of present members, and ensure funds are used for lake restoration projects, shared gear, and emergency supplies. All reserve transactions, fines, and tax contributions are posted on the communal ledger. An auditor, elected for a one‑year term by simple majority vote of the community, must have at least three years of experience in public accounting, auditing, or fisheries management, and will audit the ledger annually, posting findings on the ledger and presenting at the monthly committee meeting. The community credit is defined as one kg of fish; its value is the current market price per kg as determined by the community’s monthly market survey.
